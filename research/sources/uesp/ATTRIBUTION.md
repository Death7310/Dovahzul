# Attribution for the UESP snapshot

Everything in this folder is text copied from the Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages (UESP), <https://en.uesp.net/>, through its MediaWiki API. The files under `research/data/` are built from it.

## What is here

- One `.wikitext` file per wiki page: the page's source text at one fixed revision, byte for byte as the API returned it.
- `manifest.d/<same name>.json`: the provenance record for that page. It holds the title, revision id, revision timestamp, a revision-pinned API URL, the SHA-256 of the saved text, the licence as the wiki reported it, and when and by which script the page was fetched.
- This file.

Nothing here was edited by hand. The SHA-256 in each record matches its file (checked 2026-11-14, 331 of 331).

Where this was read:

- The wiki's API (`action=query&meta=siteinfo&siprop=rightsinfo`) returns the licence text "Attribution-ShareAlike 2.5 License" and names the policy page `UESPWiki:Copyright and Ownership`. Checked 2026-11-14.
- That policy page, <https://en.uesp.net/wiki/UESPWiki:Copyright_and_Ownership> (revision 3529700, 2026-03-26), links the two Creative Commons addresses above and sets out the wiki's reuse terms.

The policy page states three conditions for using UESP material:

1. Your materials must in turn be licensed under the by-sa licence.
2. You must give attribution to the article the content was taken from.
3. You must give access to the page where the content was used.

It adds that the last two are met by linking to the article on the site and to the article's history page. The table at the end of this file gives both links for every page, plus a link to the exact revision saved.

The same policy page says that the copyright in an edit belongs to the user who made it, so the authors to credit are the contributors listed in each article's history.

Any file, build or release that contains text from this folder, or from the data files derived from it, should carry a notice along these lines:

> Contains material from the Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages (UESP), https://en.uesp.net/, by its contributors, used under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 2.5 License (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.5/). The material has been selected and restructured. The source articles and revisions are listed in research/sources/uesp/ATTRIBUTION.md.

The wording is a suggestion; the three conditions above are what it has to satisfy. Each data file already names the page and revision id behind every record, and repeats the licence in its `meta` block.

## Points for the project owner to decide

These are observations, not legal advice.

- **Share-alike reaches the derived data.** The JSON files under `research/data/` reproduce wiki text (dictionary glosses, translations, editors' notes), so they are adaptations of BY-SA material. Condition 1 above then applies to them, and to whatever ships them. This is an inference from the licence terms; it bears on the licence the project chooses for its own data and possibly for the app.
- **Later licence versions.** Creative Commons' own comparison of licence versions (wiki.creativecommons.org, "License Versions") describes version 2.5 as letting adaptations be released under the same licence, a later version of it, or a national port. If that reading holds, releasing the derived data under CC BY-SA 3.0 or 4.0 would be allowed. This was seen only as a search-result summary, not read in the licence text itself. [UNVERIFIED]
- **The game text is not the wiki's to license.** Word-wall inscriptions, dialogue, song lyrics and book texts are quoted by the wiki from Bethesda Softworks and ZeniMax games and publications. The wiki's licence covers what its editors wrote (translations, glosses, analyses, page prose); it cannot grant rights in the quoted game text. The policy page also records that The Elder Scrolls, Skyrim and related names are trademarks of ZeniMax Media Inc. Whether the app may redistribute the quoted game text is a separate question that this snapshot does not answer.
- **Pages are not final.** A wiki page can change after the revision saved here. The revision id in each record is what was used.

## How the pages were fetched

- `tools/ingest/fetch_lexicon.py` fetched `Lore:Dragon Language` (the dictionary page).
- `tools/ingest/fetch_corpus.py` fetched the other 330 pages. Its page list was built from the links into and out of the dictionary page, 22 full-text searches and 8 categories; two pages (`Lore:Vundeheim`, `Lore:Korvag Crag`) were added on 2026-11-14 after a further search for pages that quote a word wall.
- Both scripts talk only to `https://en.uesp.net/w/api.php`, identify themselves as `research-client/1.0`, wait at least a second between calls and stop on a refusal.
